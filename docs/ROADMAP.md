# Sell Log as the recorder, not the notetaker

Log can become a paid app worth €5-10, but not by turning into another AI notetaker. By late 2026 nearly every notetaker records without a bot, and almost all of them throw the audio away. Nobody else combines what Log already does: raw audio kept as plain local files, separate aligned "me" and "them" tracks plus a stereo mix, no network use, an MIT licence and a one-off price. The paid 1.x therefore needs fewer features than it might seem. First, it must never lose a call: today an unclean exit can leave unplayable files, and calls can be recorded silently. Second, it must notice calls and handle consent in the way App Review guideline 2.5.14 and the current lawsuits against Granola and Otter demand. Third, it must pass the Mac App Store sandbox. Finally, it should add a local "Me/Them" transcript that comes almost free from the separate tracks. Direct one-off rivals sell plain recorders for $9-15 and a two-track recorder with transcription for $19.99. That supports launching at €4.99 as a recorder and moving to €7.99 once transcripts ship. The free GitHub build stays identical, as Maccy does. The plan below does this in five small releases, each shippable on its own, and leaves summaries, diarization, per-app capture and Core Audio taps for 2.x.

## Most notetakers now record without a bot and then delete the audio

The market splits into three groups, and Log competes with each one differently. **Cloud AI notetakers** charge per seat, roughly $8-40 a month. Granola Business is $14 a user a month, and its free tier keeps only 30 days of history ([Granola pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing)). Otter's free tier caps each conversation at 30 minutes ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing)). Krisp has no free plan at all ([Krisp pricing](https://krisp.ai/pricing/)). These products now record without a bot. Fathom's bot-free capture is "now live on Mac" ([Fathom What's New](https://www.fathom.ai/whats-new)), and Notion detects calls from mic use and offers to transcribe ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/ai-meeting-notes)). **Recording without a bot no longer sets a product apart.** What separates Log from these tools is what they discard. Granola deletes audio after transcription ([Granola pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing)). Jamie streams audio and stores nothing ([Reline](https://www.reline.so/blog/best-ai-notetaker-for-mac-2026)). Notion keeps a local copy for at most 24 hours unless you opt in ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/ai-meeting-notes)).

**Native Mac recorders** are the real price competitors. Rogue Amoeba sets the quality bar: Piezo costs $29 and Audio Hijack $69, both one-off ([Rogue Amoeba press kit](https://rogueamoeba.com/company/presskit/)), and their ARK capture backend needs no installer on macOS 14.4+ ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/?p=75451)). Below them sits a crowded tier of App Store apps:

- **Diphony** is free for 2 sessions a day, with a $19.99 one-off Pro. It needs macOS 15+, writes mic and system audio to separate files and transcribes on-device in English only ([diphony.com](https://diphony.com/)). It is Log's closest rival.
- **AudioRecord** is free plus an $8.99 in-app purchase, and mixes both sides into one file ([mkarim](https://www.mkarim.us/apps/audiorecord.html)).
- **Soundshine Record** is $14.99 and uses Core Audio process taps ([Soundshine](https://www.soundshine.app/record/)).
- **Replay** sells itself as a "Bot-Free Meeting Recorder for Mac, $10 Once" ([Replay](https://tryreplay.online/)).
- **Advanced Call Recorder** is reportedly $5.99 but needs a sound driver ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/advanced-call-recorder/id1604168526?mt=12)).

MacWhisper is the heavyweight local transcriber: €64 direct, and $90-100 lifetime on the App Store, where it **lacks automatic meeting detection** ([usevoicy](https://usevoicy.com/blog/macwhisper-pricing/)).

**Built-in platform recording** has gaps that Log fills:

- **Google Meet** records only on paid Workspace or Google One 2 TB+ plans, never on free Gmail ([Google Meet help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9308681)).
- **Teams** needs a work licence and an admin policy, and external participants can't record ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/meeting-recording)).
- **Zoom** allows local recording on its free plan, but participants must ask the host for permission ([Zoom support](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0063640)).
- **Slack huddles** produce no native recording file ([recall.ai](https://www.recall.ai/post/capturing-slack-huddle-data-with-recall-ai)).
- **Apple's Phone app on macOS Tahoe** records only relayed phone calls and FaceTime audio, and plays an audible notice to both sides ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/phoneapp/while-on-a-call-phn4828a6/mac)).

The one free threat to watch is macOS 27's screenshot recorder (⌘⇧5). It reportedly gains "Include System Audio", but it still outputs a single video file, and only one vendor source reports the feature ([meetingsrecorder.com](https://meetingsrecorder.com/blog/record-system-audio-mac/)).

## Log's defensible ground is raw local stems that nobody else ships

ScreenCaptureKit delivers system audio and mic audio as separate streams but cannot split participants. Recall.ai, which sells per-participant capture to developers, says so itself ([Recall blog](https://www.recall.ai/blog/how-to-use-screencapturekit-to-record-a-meeting)). Log's me/them/stereo split is therefore the ceiling of what plain capture can give, and Log already ships it. Diphony stops at two unmixed files ([diphony.com](https://diphony.com/)), AudioRecord mixes everything into one file, and the cloud tools keep no stems at all. **The empty spot in the market is raw, aligned, local stems with a one-off price and an open licence.** No major tool sits there.

Four further advantages defend that position. First, the plain-files design answers the Rewind shutdown. Meta bought Limitless, disabled Rewind's Mac capture on 2025-12-19, and left users with only "export or delete" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/)). That made "my files, in a folder, readable without the app" a selling point. Second, the 1 fps screen capture fills a gap that nobody else covers since Rewind died: audio-first recorders don't capture screens, and screen recorders shoot full video. Third, the MIT licence matters at work. Hacker News users rejected a GPL notetaker as "dead in the water for using something like this at work" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44725306)). Fourth, the separate tracks give Log answers to the two hardest complaints about local tools. Speaker labels come free for 1:1 calls, and echo can be removed after the call because the "them" track is a perfect, time-aligned reference ([Recall.ai AEC](https://www.recall.ai/blog/acoustic-echo-cancellation)).

The position is weaker in one respect. Log is not a notetaker, and buyers who want summaries will choose Granola or MacWhisper. At least six Reddit users describe their own pipeline: record locally, then hand the file to a chatbot or local LLM ([MetaWhisp](https://metawhisp.com/blog/best-ai-note-taker-reddit/)). Log should serve that "record, then my own AI" user with an agent-friendly folder, and should not compete on summary quality, which draws complaints against every vendor.

## Users pay for "it definitely recorded" and "no subscription"

The complaint data points to reliability and trust, not features. In one tally of 116 Granola verdicts on Reddit, 8 complaints were about reliability: calls that "looked recorded but weren't", a crash that lost a meeting. Another 8 were about speaker labels, and 4 were about missing audio ([MetaWhisp Granola](https://metawhisp.com/blog/granola-reddit-reviews/)). A guide to MacWhisper warns that its auto-recording can fail, so users should keep a backup ([whispernotes](https://whispernotes-web.pages.dev/blog/offline-meeting-transcription-mac)). One reviewer tried eight open-source recorders, returned all of them, and cited "crashes, hangs, setup that feels like configuring a 1998 Linux desktop" ([ax0x](https://blog.ax0x.ai/meeting-recorder-oss-survey-v2)). For a €5 recorder, **one lost call is a refund and a one-star review**, so the first paid release has to be hardened first.

Requested features follow a clear order. Speaker separation is called a "show stopper" and a "dealbreaker" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44725306)). Next come automatic meeting detection, so users don't forget to press record, and Markdown saved "into a folder of my choice" ([HN comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44728215)). Price complaints are about minute caps and credits, not the amount itself: 14 Reddit commenters raised them ([MetaWhisp](https://metawhisp.com/blog/best-ai-note-taker-reddit/)). Two hardware problems also recur:

- **Echo.** Without headphones, the far end leaks into the mic and gets transcribed twice ([Plaud support](https://support.plaud.ai/hc/en-us/articles/53794941547673-Echo-when-recording-with-Plaud-Desktop)).
- **AirPods.** Opening an AirPods mic forces Bluetooth into its low-quality call mode for both playback and mic ([Liscio](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/2021/01/28/more-on-avaudioengine-airpods/)). Log already avoids this by preferring the built-in mic.

Consent is now a commercial risk, not just an ethical one. A 2026 class action against Granola argues that silent capture without a bot breaks the federal Wiretap Act and California's CIPA, because "no bot joins the meeting and no notification appears". Granola's notice features were off by default ([Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/what-the-granola-class-action-means-for-companies-building-and-deploying-conversation-capture-tools)). The Otter suit targets recording that started without anyone's explicit consent ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/otter-transcription-ai-training-lawsuit/)). Nine US states require consent from all parties ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/ai-notetaking-poses-wiretapping-discovery-and-ethical-pitfalls)). App Review guideline 2.5.14 independently requires "explicit user consent" and "a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple has rejected at least one app because its indicator could be turned off ([Apple Dev Forums 654120](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/654120)).

## The feature spec: eleven must-haves for paid 1.x, the rest wait

Each feature in the table below is the smallest version that satisfies its evidence. "Done when" is the test the release must pass. The API column names the system framework to use. Every 1.x must-have uses system frameworks only, so `build.sh` stays dependency-free.

### Must-have for the paid 1.x

| # | Feature | Why | Done when | API |

| M1 | **Crash-safe tracks** | `Log.swift` writes AAC `.m4a` through `AVAudioFile`, and an MP4-family file is unplayable if the app dies before finalising ([WWDC20 10011](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10011/)). The "looked recorded but wasn't" complaint is the top trust killer. | `kill -9` at minute 10, then relaunch. | M9 | **Sandboxed App Store build and notarized DMG** | 2.4.5(i) requires the sandbox. 5.1.1(i) requires a privacy policy link. M10 is a must-have for 1.x but not for the first App Store release; the roadmap below places it.

Apple's SpeechTranscriber is chosen over Parakeet or Whisper for 1.x for four reasons. It needs no dependency. It adds nothing to app size. It avoids the gray zone in guideline 2.4.5(iv) on downloading "additional code, or resources" after install ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). And its accuracy is good enough: 14.0 WER against whisper-small's 12.8 on earnings calls in a vendor benchmark ([Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax)), and better than Whisper Small on LibriSpeech in an independent test ([rohitraj.tech](https://rohitraj.tech/notes/apple-speechanalyzer-vs-whisper-on-device-stt-2026)). The costs are 10 languages, no custom vocabulary, and macOS 26 only.

Some features should not be built at all: a cloud service, accounts, sync, a meeting bot, live captions, a library window or editor, format choices, or a dedicated MCP server. A folder of `.m4a`, `meta.json` and `transcript.md` files can already be read by any filesystem MCP server or coding agent. A bundled MCP process would also run into the 2.4.5(iii) rule against processes that keep running after the user quits ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

## Five small releases put a hardened recorder on the App Store before adding transcripts

Each phase ships on its own, has a terminal test where possible, and keeps the single-file design for as long as it holds. The transcriber is the first code that justifies a second file.

App Store compliance work belongs in v1.3, not v1.1. The first two releases change capture and file handling, and sandboxing a still-moving target doubles the debugging. Two choices made earlier keep v1.3 cheap. M1 and M7 keep writing to `~/Downloads/Log/`, which the Downloads entitlement allows, so no security-scoped bookmark code is needed. M10 uses the speech model the OS downloads, so the 2.4.5(iv) download question never arises in 1.x. Open at login already defaults to off, which satisfies the 2.4.5(iii) consent rule. Updates are separate by rule: 2.4.5(vii) says App Store apps update only through the App Store, so the GitHub DMG keeps GitHub Releases and gains no updater code.

## Price at €4.99, then €7.99, with one identical paid app and no in-app purchases

Maccy is the closest precedent: MIT source on GitHub, a functionally identical $9.99 App Store build sold "to support the development", and free Homebrew installs alongside ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/maccy/id1527619437); [methodshop](https://methodshop.com/maccy-clipboard-manager/)). Buyers pay for convenience: signing, sandboxing, automatic updates and supporting the developer. They don't pay because features are locked away. This suits a small codebase, because there is no StoreKit code, no licence checks (which 2.4.5(vi) forbids anyway), and no feature flags to keep in sync between the two builds.

At **€4.99 for v1.3**, Log sits below its recorder peers: AudioRecord's $8.99 add-on, Replay's $10 and Soundshine's $14.99. It still offers more than they do: three aligned files, screen capture and call detection. At **€7.99 once transcripts ship in v1.4**, it still undercuts Diphony's $19.99 for a comparable two-track-plus-transcript feature set ([diphony.com](https://diphony.com/)), and it is a twelfth of MacWhisper's App Store lifetime price. Early buyers get the transcripts free, which rewards them and gives the launch a story. Apple's Small Business Program fee is 15% ([SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/google-play-apple-app-store-fees/)). A subscription can't be justified: the usual rule is that recurring pricing needs "servers, sync, storage, API calls" ([Ziyarex](https://ziyarex.com/journal/pricing-a-12-dollar-mac-app)), and Log has none of them. "No subscription, no minute caps" also answers the most common price complaint directly. If 2.0's Parakeet and diarization prove expensive to maintain, the simplest next step is a paid "Log 2" app at €9.99, not in-app purchases.

Be honest about expectations. One-time revenue is "lumpy, busy at launch, quiet between releases" ([Ziyarex](https://ziyarex.com/journal/pricing-a-12-dollar-mac-app)). Neither the notes nor any public source give revenue figures for Maccy or similar apps. At these prices, treat Log as a self-funding side project, not a business. State the monetisation plan openly in the README: Hyprnote's launch thread was asked three separate times how it would make money ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44725306)).

## Risks, open questions and research gaps

**App Review is the largest unknown.** The notes found no confirmed list of approved App Store meeting recorders that use ScreenCaptureKit. The evidence is an Apple forum reply saying "a lot of apps on the App Store" use ScreenCaptureKit ([Apple Dev Forums 728140](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/728140)) and Diphony's presence on the store with a similar design. Before investing in v1.3, inspect the entitlements of shipping recorders with `codesign -d --entitlements`. There is also a design tension. Log deliberately keeps its menu bar icon discreet on shared screens, but 2.5.14 wants a "clear" indication that can't be disabled. The filled icon probably satisfies it, but prepare a red-dot variant in case it's rejected.

**Call detection inside the sandbox is unverified.** Whether the Core Audio process-object properties are readable from a sandboxed app is untested. MacWhisper's App Store edition lacking detection is a warning sign ([usevoicy](https://usevoicy.com/blog/macwhisper-pricing/)). If it fails, M5 becomes a GitHub-only feature, and the App Store listing must not promise it. Spike this before starting v1.2.

**Crash-safe CAF is an inference.** Claims that CAF holding AAC can be recovered after `kill -9` come from general knowledge, not the notes. M1's acceptance test settles the question, and the `movieFragmentInterval` route is the fallback with an Apple source behind it.

**Competitive and legal pressure.** If macOS 27's ⌘⇧5 really records system audio, "good enough" capture becomes free. Log's answer is separate audio-only stems, detection and transcripts, not capture itself. The consent features reduce the reputational risk the Granola suit shows, but they don't move legal responsibility away from the user. The README and the App Store copy must say so. EU and UK GDPR rules for personal call recording were not researched.

**Remaining gaps:**

- Whether the System Audio Recording permission for taps also re-prompts periodically. This decides how much L1 is worth.
- Whether macOS 26 changed the monthly Screen Recording re-prompt.
- Whether `SCRecordingOutput` screen files survive a crash. M1 covers audio only.
- Current App Store prices for Call Record and Advanced Call Recorder (the store returned HTTP 429).
- How SpeechTranscriber handles accents and domain terms, the main reason one reviewer returned all eight open-source tools ([ax0x](https://blog.ax0x.ai/meeting-recorder-oss-survey-v2)).
- Whether App Intents can be built without Xcode.
- First-hand Reddit evidence. Most of it reached the notes through competitor blogs, so treat counts such as "8 reliability complaints" as directional.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the obvious plan. A notetaker-shaped roadmap would chase summaries, diarization and integrations, which cloud tools with venture funding already do adequately and get criticised for anyway. Log's buyers have a different worry: did it actually record, are both sides there, and are the files mine? The most valuable work for the next three releases is therefore invisible: crash-safe files, failure handling, a health check, and a detection prompt that also covers consent. Transcripts are a near-free bonus, because the me/them split that Log already ships is the hard part of speaker labelling.

The lazy path is also the defensible one. Every 1.x must-have uses system frameworks, writes to the folder Log already uses, and avoids the model downloads, licence keys, background processes and update channels that cause trouble in App Review. If the work holds to that discipline, a paid, App Store-compliant Log with transcripts should fit, by this report's own rough estimate, in about a thousand lines of Swift with no dependencies, and that small size is itself part of the pitch to the open-source audience that will find it first.
